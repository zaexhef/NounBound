# Puzzle Bible

## Golden rule

Every intended connection must be specific, defensible, consistently phrased, and stronger as a four-word group than any accidental alternative.

## Supported domains

Celebrities, automotive, everyday items, history, music, sports, film/TV, science, geography, food, mythology, games, Detroit culture, Black history, and carefully sourced crime history.

## Construction process

1. Write four connection labels.
2. Add four nouns per label.
3. List every alternate meaning for every noun.
4. Search for unintended groups of three or four.
5. Adjust decoys so ambiguity challenges but does not invalidate.
6. Write staged hints and explanations.
7. Fact-check spelling, identity, dates, and claims.
8. Run schema validation.
9. Conduct blind playtests.
10. Mark approved only after editorial sign-off.

## Naming rules

- Prefer a single noun or short noun phrase.
- Keep display text concise enough for small phones.
- No two visible cards may have identical normalized text.
- Avoid punctuation unless essential to the proper noun.
- Specify context in the group label: “U.S. presidents,” not merely “leaders.”
- Do not rely on pronunciation unless the puzzle explicitly signals wordplay.

## Ambiguity policy

Good ambiguity:
- Ford: automaker, president, actor surname
- Mercury: vehicle marque, planet, element, Roman deity

Unfair ambiguity:
- Regional slang unknown outside one neighborhood
- A private nickname with no reliable source
- A connection depending on disputed trivia
- Multiple complete partitions of equal strength with no clue

If a legitimate alternate group exists, revise the board or record it as a Clever Connection. Clever Connections can award recognition but do not silently alter the intended solution.

## Difficulty rubric

| Factor | Easy | Normal | Hard | Expert |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Familiarity | universal | common | mixed | specialized but sourceable |
| Overlap | 0–1 decoys | 1–2 | several | pervasive |
| Relationship | direct | direct/compound | indirect | collision/context shift |
| Label | obvious | inferable | precise | precise and surprising |

## Editorial status

`draft` → `fact_checked` → `ambiguity_reviewed` → `playtested` → `approved` → `published` → `retired`

Only approved content ships.

## Playtest acceptance

- At least three blind testers for easy/normal and five for hard/expert.
- Majority reaches intended solution without being told the category.
- Testers rate the reveal as fair.
- Any repeated confusion is a content defect, not a player defect.
- Record completion rate, mistakes, hints, solve time, and alternate guesses.

## Sensitive content

- Avoid living-person rumors and defamatory implications.
- Use neutral wording for crime/history topics.
- Do not trivialize victims, atrocities, protected identities, or traumatic events.
- Provide content warnings for mature packs if ever introduced.
- Use licensed or original media only; the base game relies on words and original UI.

## Puzzle quality checklist

- [ ] Exactly 16 unique cards
- [ ] Exactly four groups of four
- [ ] Every noun belongs to exactly one intended group
- [ ] Label and explanation match all four nouns
- [ ] Hints progress without spoiling immediately
- [ ] No accidental full solution of equal strength
- [ ] Facts verified
- [ ] Difficulty calibrated
- [ ] Small-screen text checked
- [ ] Review status approved
